Typography:

Visual Hierarchy:

The first idea I wanted to exercise here is visual hierarchy, which lets people who visit the website know what to look at first. Size, weight, spacing, and font choice create visual hierarchy. Headlines should stand out the most so a page can be quickly scanned.

Contrast:

The second idea that I exercise here is contrast. Decorative fonts are used to get attention and are best in headings, but body text should be a font that is easy to read. This helps distinguish the content on your website. To learn more, read this:



One of these ways to choose fonts is to choose a pair of opposites. A good example of this is to use a big and bold serif for your headline and a nice traditional serif typeface for the body copy. Take a look at this example, to see this tip in action.

Color and Design:

Color Contrast:

Good color contrast helps users read text more easily. Light text on a light background and dark text on a dark background can be difficult to read, especially if users are used to having a device with low brightness. Having a guideline like this for accessibility encourages sufficient contrast.


Color Psychology

For color, color psychology is probably the most obvious design element I've employed here. Colors communicate different moods and feelings. Warm colors such as orange often feel energetic while cooler colors like blue and green are calmer. In my case, I want to start off the website with something more energetic so people would be encouraged to view it, but if you wanted a cooler mood so people would really take time to soak in the information, use something like blue.
